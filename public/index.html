August 2026 research sprint

Evidence before automation.

The THA Q&A Hub is evaluated as a privacy-aware, human-in-the-loop retrieval assistant—not an autonomous professor. Every draft remains reviewable and unpublished until a professor approves it.

Current conclusionPromising prototype

Technical feasibility demonstrated. Human effectiveness, legal readiness, and production WhatsApp reliability still require institutional evaluation.

Research question

Can verified retrieval reduce repetitive course communication without reducing professor control?

The study separates retrieval quality, unsupported-question abstention, answer grounding, human editing effort, usability, privacy, and operational risk.

Key finding

Retrieval presence is not evidence sufficiency.

The original keyword retriever found superficially related passages for five of six unsupported questions. A documented evidence threshold and transparent synonym handling eliminated those failures on the 15-case development benchmark. Because the intervention followed baseline inspection, the perfect guarded result is not proof of generalisation.

What is proven

  • Local course-document retrieval is reproducible.
  • Sources remain visible to the professor.
  • Unsupported questions trigger explicit abstention.
  • Publishing and WhatsApp delivery require professor action.
  • All current application safety tests pass.

What is not proven

  • No real professor workload study has occurred.
  • No student usability study has occurred.
  • No claim of DSGVO compliance is made.
  • No production OpenWA bridge has been fault-tested.
  • The synthetic benchmark is not independent evidence.
